## Target reset on Aug 7 — and why the training barely changes

The old plan trained at 4:44/km for a 3:20 finish. Thursday's session showed 4:44/km currently costs 169–177 bpm against a threshold heart rate of 171, which means it was threshold pace, not marathon pace — sustainable for an hour, not three and a half. Moving to 4:55/km puts marathon-pace work back where it physiologically belongs. Every session structure below is unchanged; only the target pace moved, by eleven seconds per kilometre.

## What four Half Ironmans say about Sep 6 — added Aug 10

Across 2022–2025 the bike split got faster every single year and the run split got slower every single year. That is not a running fitness problem, it is a pacing problem: the bike leg is being ridden at a cost the run then pays, and the fade always starts in the middle of the half marathon rather than at the transition. Two consequences for this plan. First, the fix is aerobic durability and bike restraint, not more short bricks — so the W3 and W4 bricks are long and run at genuine race effort, and everything else off the bike stays easy. Second, on race day the bike leg is the discipline test: ride it slower than you can, so that the run is something other than damage control.

## Standing rules, learned in July–August 2026

1. HRV gates every quality day. Unbalanced-low means the session moves, it does not shrink.
2. No alcohol the night before a quality or long session.
3. If a quality session slips a day, the long run slips with it. Never run hard and long back to back — that is what cost 4 km on Aug 7.
4. Hard days are scheduled, never spontaneous. Easy means HR under 140, no exceptions.
5. Quality sessions are gated on two nights above 7 h with overnight HRV at or above 37. Below that, the session goes easy — it does not get substituted for something harder.
6. A prescribed pace is a ceiling, not a floor. Three sessions in a row (Aug 6, 7, 11) were run faster or longer than written; that is the same pattern that cost July.
