## The gap I cannot close: no outdoor power meter

Your power curve came back empty across every outdoor ride — only Zwift sessions carry watts. That matters twice. First, a Zwift ramp test computes FTP from your best one-minute effort, which flatters riders with strong anaerobic punch, so 245 W may read optimistic outdoors. Second, and more practically, you cannot pace this race by power because you will have no power on race day. Plan to ride to heart rate instead: hold 140–148 bpm and treat anything above 150 as borrowing from your run split. On the May 28 Sevan loop you averaged 150 bpm for seven and a half hours, so that ceiling is well within reach for ninety minutes less than half the distance.

That is the single best bike predictor a coach could ask for. The bike leg is half your race, and it is the half we can forecast with real confidence.

## What could make it faster

- A calm morning. Wind is the single biggest variable on this course.
- You are already altitude-adapted relative to most of the field — you train at 950–1,000 m, not sea level.
- The bike course is flat. Your climbing-heavy training rides understate what you can do on the shoreline.
- Four more weeks of run fitness on a rising curve.

## What could make it slower

- Zero brick sessions so far. The run split is the least certain number in this whole prediction.
- No swim in your recent data. 45–50 min is your history, not a measured current figure.
- 1,900 m hurts the run more than the bike. Expect HR to sit higher than usual for the same pace.
- The marathon plan has deliberately cut long-ride volume. Agreed trade, but this is where you pay it.

1. **Confirm the actual race date.** Planning to Sunday Sep 6, but the event site shows a late-August date on one schedule page. The whole taper hangs off that.
